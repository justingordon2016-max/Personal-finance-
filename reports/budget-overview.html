Your salary lands on the 25th. By the 18th of the next month, R57,374 of it has gone on things you cannot change this month — loans, cover, connectivity, the weekly subscription, bank fees. That leaves R12,626.

What to change, in order

1. Right-size the savings. R2,300 a month is what salary supports. The R9,000 transfer is the flexible one — the TFSA at R3,006 has an annual limit worth using, and FNB Invest is R2,500. Cutting the R9,000 to about R2,300 closes the gap on its own, and you would still be saving R7,800 a month.

2. Move the date. The R9,000 leaves on the 27th, four days before R27,813 of month-end debits. Move it to the 4th, after the 1st-of-month run, so it can never be the thing that tips you overdrawn.

3. Then attack fixed. R7,976 a month of life and risk cover across two providers, R5,804 of connectivity across eight debits, and a R6,144 weekly subscription are the three reviewable blocks. Anything you free up there goes straight back into savings.

How this was built. All 192 transactions from six FNB statements — cheque 135–137 and card 130–132 — were transcribed to data/transactions.csv and data/card-transactions.csv and categorised by hand, with JAC confirmed as insurance and FWT as an EasyEquities tax-free savings contribution. scripts/analyze.py and scripts/analyze_card.py check every cycle against the opening and closing balances printed on the statements: all three cheque cycles and both facilities on all three card statements tie to R0.00. The fixed schedule on this page lives in data/monthly-commitments.csv at current rates, and scripts/monthly_plan.py produces the walk-through above.

Fixed versus variable. Fixed means a standing instruction at a rate you cannot change this month. Prepaid electricity and airtime are treated as variable — they recur, but you choose when and how much. Savings debit orders are shown separately from fixed costs throughout, because they are money you keep, not money you spend.

What this does not cover. Savings, investment, structured-loan and vehicle-finance balances, so the principal you repay each month — real, and positive for net worth — is not counted anywhere. The two accounts also run on different cycles (the card closes around the 29th, the cheque account on the 17th), so combined monthly figures are close but not exact, and R5,000 of card payments does not reconcile between them. Nothing here is financial advice — it is your own data, added up.

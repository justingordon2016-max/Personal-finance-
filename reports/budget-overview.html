What the account actually did

Cheque balance · 16 May – 17 Sep 2026

Four cycles of the same shape. The buffer fell from R49,838 to R4,677 over the first three, and the account was overdrawn for five days in late July. September closed up, at R8,937 — but only because R30,000 came in from outside on 20 August. On salary alone that cycle would have closed about R25,740 down. Two large external transfers in one month, R30,000 and R200,000, mean the budget above describes what things cost, not how the account is currently being funded.

Where the numbers come from. data/budget.csv is the budget: every line at its current rate, classified fixed, variable or savings. scripts/budget.py produces this page's figures from it. Behind that, data/transactions.csv and data/card-transactions.csv hold all 236 transactions from seven statements — cheque 135–138 and card 130–132 — and scripts/analyze.py and scripts/analyze_card.py check every cycle against the opening and closing balances printed on them. All four cheque cycles and both facilities on all three card statements tie to R0.00.

How fixed and variable are decided. Fixed means a standing instruction at a rate you cannot change this month, listed at its most recent amount rather than an average. Variable means you choose when and how much, so those lines are four-cycle averages — prepaid electricity and airtime sit here for that reason, even though they recur. Savings are standing instructions too, but kept in their own column because they are money retained rather than spent. Payments to the second credit account are shown as variable because their timing and size are irregular; if that account turns out to carry a fixed instalment, R7,750 moves columns and the shape of this budget changes.

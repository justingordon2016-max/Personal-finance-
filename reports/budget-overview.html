The problem is liquidity, and now it is structural too

You closed August at R4,677 against R66,100 of debit orders that hit on the 25th and the 1st. In late July the account was already overdrawn for five days, bottoming around R3,200 below zero before payday, and you paid overdraft interest plus a R35 fee for it.

What the card statements add is that this is not a one-month squeeze you can wait out. With the subscription and the iStore instalment counted, recurring commitments are R73,017 against a R70,000 salary. The gap does not close on its own — and there is no longer a buffer absorbing it.

How this was built. All 192 transactions from six FNB statements — cheque 135–137 and card 130–132 — were transcribed to data/transactions.csv and data/card-transactions.csv and categorised by hand. scripts/analyze.py and scripts/analyze_card.py check every cycle against the opening and closing balances printed on the statements: all three cheque cycles and both facilities on all three card statements tie to R0.00, and the cheque transaction counts match the turnover lines (45 / 45 / 50). Category assignments are judgement calls — edit the category column and re-run to change any of them.

What this does not cover. The savings and investment account balances, and the structured-loan and vehicle-finance balances — so the principal you repay each month, which is real and positive, is not counted anywhere on this page. The cheque and card statements also run on different cycles (the card closes around the 29th, the cheque account on the 17th), so combined monthly figures are close but not exact. R5,000 of card payments does not reconcile between the two accounts. Nothing here is financial advice — it is your own data, added up.

Your salary is R70,000 a month, steady across all three cycles. Fixed debit orders take R66,100 of it before you buy a single thing — leaving about R3,900 for food, fuel, family and everything else.

That gap is why the cash buffer went from R49,838 to R4,677 in three months, and why the account sat overdrawn for five days in late July.

What actually happened to your buffer

The R45,161 question

The headline looks alarming — R45,161 of cash gone in three months — but the money did not evaporate. Almost all of it went somewhere useful:

So your net worth improved by roughly R14,110 a month: you converted a cash pile into a smaller credit-card balance and a bigger savings balance. That is a good trade on paper.

The problem is liquidity, not solvency

The cushion that made that trade possible is now spent. You closed August at R4,677 against R66,100 of debit orders that hit on the 25th and the 1st. In late July the account was already overdrawn for five days, bottoming around R3,200 below zero before payday, and you paid overdraft interest plus a R35 fee for it.

There is no longer any margin for a burst geyser, a car repair or a late salary. The next unexpected R10,000 has to go on the credit card or the R20,000 overdraft facility — which starts the cycle over at 21%.

You are saving into an overdraft Timing

How this was built. All 140 transactions from FNB statements 135, 136 and 137 were transcribed to data/transactions.csv and categorised by hand. scripts/analyze.py checks each cycle against the opening and closing balances printed on the statement: all three tie to R0.00, and the transaction counts match the turnover lines (45 / 45 / 50). Category assignments are judgement calls — edit the category column and re-run to change any of them.

What this does not cover. Credit-card spending, the savings and investment account balances, the structured-loan and vehicle-finance balances, and any account other than the cheque account. This is a cash-flow view of one account, not a full financial position. Nothing here is financial advice — it is your own data, added up.
